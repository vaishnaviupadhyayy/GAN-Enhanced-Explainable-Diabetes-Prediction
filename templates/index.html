GAN vs Original Dataset: Model Performance Comparison

All models were evaluated on the same untouched test set of 154 records. The original training set contained 614 records. The GAN-augmented training set contained 814 records, including 200 synthetic diabetic-class records.

Experimental Findings

Best overall result: Random Forest trained on the original dataset achieved the highest accuracy (77.92%) and F1-score (65.31%) in this experiment.

Effect of GAN augmentation: The augmented data improved F1-score for Logistic Regression and slightly for SVM, but reduced the Random Forest and XGBoost results. Therefore, GAN augmentation did not improve overall model performance in this experiment.

Interpretation: Synthetic data increased the number of diabetic-class training records, but adding synthetic records did not consistently improve predictive performance. The quality and distribution of generated records need further evaluation.

These results are from one train-test split and should not be interpreted as clinical performance. The GAN-generated records were checked for feature-range validity, but this alone does not establish that their distributions are realistic. Further validation, cross-validation and evaluation on independent data are required.

Important Limitations

The training dataset contains female participants only. The model has not been clinically validated and may produce incorrect predictions.

SHAP and LIME explain model behaviour; they do not establish medical causation.

This research prototype cannot diagnose diabetes. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for appropriate clinical testing.
